Operating manual & walkthrough · neXgen Multispecialty Healthcare, New Delhi

One OPD queue, four people who need it to work.

neXgen Healthcare is an outpatient appointment and patient-history hub. A patient reserves an OPD slot online for ₹50 and settles the consultant's fee at the hospital; the consultant sees a live queue with the full history attached; the front desk keeps the roster and the money straight; the administrator sets the rules everyone else works inside. This manual covers all four, feature by feature, and takes the booking flow apart step by step. Every screenshot in it was captured from the running application.

Signs in as

Patient

Books and reschedules slots, joins video calls, keeps records and invoices, sets reminders.

Signs in as

Consultant

Works the day's queue, reads history, writes prescriptions, closes consultations.

Signs in as

Front Desk

Registers patients, books on their behalf, collects fees, manages consultant availability.

Signs in as

Administrator

Sets hospital details and tax, toggles SMS and telehealth, reseeds the system.

Part One

The system at a glance

Five ideas explain nearly everything the software does. Read these once and the rest of the manual will follow without surprises.

Slots have capacity, not just a time

Every consultant publishes a list of time slots and a maximum number of patients per slot. Dr. Rajesh Sharma runs six slots with three patients each; Dr. Rohan Kapoor runs four slots with two each. A slot shows 3 left until it fills, then Full. The server counts every non-cancelled booking for that exact consultant, date and slot, and refuses the booking if the count has already reached the cap — so two people cannot take the last place at the same moment.

Money splits in two

The ₹50 booking fee is paid online and holds the slot. The consultant's fee — ₹750 to ₹1,300 depending on the specialist — is paid at the hospital reception on the day. Marking a visit as a follow-up takes 20% off the consultant's fee, never off the ₹50. The confirmation screen prints both numbers so the patient knows exactly what cash to carry.

An appointment moves through four states

StateSet whenWhat it means on screen
scheduled Booking confirmed Appears in the patient's upcoming list and the consultant's queue.
active-video Either side joins the video room A pulsing Live badge appears in the sidebar under “In Session”.
completed Consultant closes the consultation Drops to the bottom of the queue; prescription is filed to the vault.
cancelled Anyone cancels Frees the place and alerts everyone waitlisted for that exact slot.

Cancelling is what feeds the waitlist

When a booking is cancelled, the system immediately finds every waitlist entry for that consultant, date and slot, flips it from waiting to notified, and raises an in-app alert. Anyone sitting on the booking screen sees a green “A slot just opened up” banner within ten seconds. Nothing is reserved for them — the banner's Take this slot button just pre-fills the booking form. First to confirm wins.

The Document Vault is the shared spine

Lab reports, referrals, vaccine cards and prescriptions all live in one vault, filed against a patient. Anything filed there is visible to that patient, to the consultant seeing them, and to the desk. When a consultant closes a consultation, the prescription is written into the vault automatically — that is what the patient downloads afterwards.

Part Three

Booking an appointment, end to end

Patient Front Desk Admin

The central act of the product. Three steps of input, one confirmation screen, and a capacity check at the server that decides whether the booking exists. Consultants do not book; they receive.

Left rail → Scheduling → Book Appointment

Step 1 — Choose a doctor

A search box matches on name, speciality or hospital. A department dropdown lists only the departments actually staffed. More filters opens three more: state, city, and consultation type (video / hospital visit only).

Each result card shows the photo, name, speciality, location, star rating, years of experience, the consultant's fee, and a Video available tag where telehealth is offered. Tap a card to select it — a tick appears in the corner — then Continue.

If nothing matches

You get a Clear filters button rather than an empty page. Changing filters so that the currently selected consultant disappears silently moves the selection to the first remaining result.

Step 2 — Date, time and patient

Four decisions on one screen, top to bottom.

  1. How would you like to consult?

    Visit the hospital or Video call. The video option is disabled and greyed out for consultants who do not offer it — Dr. Vikramaditya Verma and Dr. Arvind Rao, in the seeded roster.

  2. Pick a day

    Seven tiles: today, tomorrow, then five weekdays by name. There is no way to book further than a week out.

  3. Pick a time

    Each slot tile carries a live count — 3 left, 1 left, Full. Counts reload whenever you change consultant or date. Selecting a full slot opens the waitlist panel instead of blocking you.

  4. Who is this appointment for?

    Choose a saved profile — which auto-fills name, age and mobile — or Someone new and type all three. Name, age and mobile are all required to continue.

The waitlist branch

When the slot you picked is full, an amber panel appears with Join the waitlist, plus a count of how many people are already waiting. Joining needs the patient name filled in first. After that, the loop below runs on its own.

Demo  A Simulate a cancellation button sits inside the same panel so you can watch the whole loop run without a second browser: it cancels a real booking in that slot and fires the alerts.

Step 3 — Review and pay

A four-row summary — doctor, when, where, patient — with an Edit link back to step 2. Below it, everything optional is folded behind one disclosure so the default path stays short.

Add reports or notes — optional

  • This is a follow-up visit — takes 20% off the consultant's fee and reveals a field for the previous booking ID.
  • Test reports and Past prescriptions — PDF, JPG, PNG or DOC. Demo Only the file names travel with the booking; the file contents are not uploaded. To attach a real document, use the Document Vault (Part 8).
  • What brings you in? — free text that lands in the consultant's notes field for this appointment.
  • How urgent is it? — Routine, Urgent, or Emergency.

How would you like to pay the ₹50?

UPI (GPay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM), Card, Net banking from five banks, or At the counter — which still holds the slot immediately and asks you to pay at reception quoting your booking number.

The payment box then states both figures plainly: ₹50 now, and the consultant's fee to carry to the hospital — struck through and reduced if the follow-up box is ticked. Underneath the confirm button: free cancellation up to 2 hours before.

Step 4 — Confirmed

The confirmation screen is also the receipt and the settings page for the visit.

Booking ID
Format BK-NXG-######, with a copy button. Quote it at reception and when rescheduling.
Arrival instruction
Arrive 15 minutes early, and the exact rupee amount to carry for the consultant's fee.
Reminders
Choose the timing — 1 hour, 3 hours, 12 hours or a day before — and the channel: WhatsApp, SMS, Email or in-app. Demo Send a test reminder shows the message as an on-screen toast; nothing is actually dispatched.
On the day
A live status line for your consultant — running on time, 20 minutes behind, 45 minutes behind, or called to emergency duty — with a toggle to be alerted if the wait exceeds 15 minutes. Demo The two Simulate buttons drive this status locally.
Tax invoice
A full printable invoice — hospital name and GSTIN, invoice number INV-BK-NXG-######, doctor block, patient and schedule block, and the amount paid.
No OPD token is issued at booking

The wizard generates a token number but the server does not store it, so the confirmation screen shows the booking ID alone — there is no token to quote. A token is only ever rendered by the front desk's check-in QR pass, and it is derived from the appointment ID as Token #BK-<last 4>. Patients should quote the booking ID at reception.

Two variants of the same wizard

Rescheduling

Opened from the Care Dashboard, the wizard launches as a modal titled Reschedule appointment, jumps straight to step 2, and carries the patient details, consultant and appointment type across. The notes field is stamped [Rescheduled from #BK-…] and the follow-up box is pre-ticked.

The old booking is cancelled only once the new one is confirmed — which also releases the old slot to its waitlist.

Booking on someone's behalf

The front desk and admin see the same Book Appointment item in the rail and use the identical wizard. At step 2 they either pick the patient from the saved list or choose Someone new and type the details.

If the name and mobile match an existing profile, the booking attaches to it; otherwise a new patient profile is created automatically at confirmation.

What can go wrong, and what the message means

MessageCauseWhat to do
Please choose a doctor to continue. Step 1 was left without a selection. Tap a consultant card.
Please choose a time for the appointment. No slot selected, or the date was changed after selecting one — changing date clears the slot. Re-select a time.
Please fill in the patient name, age and mobile number. One of the three required patient fields is empty. Complete all three.
Slot is fully booked. Only N schedules allowed per slot for Dr. X. The slot filled between loading the screen and confirming. Go back a step, pick another time, or join the waitlist.
Network communication failure. The API did not respond. Check the server, then retry — nothing was saved.
Part Four

Patient manual

Patient

Patients see three destinations in the left rail: Persona Portal, Book Appointment and Document Vault. The portal is the home base and splits into three tabs.

Left rail → Care → Persona Portal

Tab 1 — Upcoming appointments & reminders

Each upcoming visit is a card carrying the consultant, date, slot, type and status, plus:

  • Set Reminder / Change — opens a panel with five timings (15 minutes, 1 hour, 3 hours, 12 hours, 1 day before) and four channels (WhatsApp, SMS, Email, browser push), with fields for the mobile number and email to use. A Test Alert button confirms the settings. Demo
  • Pay Fee (₹…) — opens the secure checkout with the consultation fee, 18% GST and total, and card fields.
  • Join Room — appears only on video appointments; takes you into the telehealth cabin (Part 9).

Below the cards, a table lists every upcoming visit across all family profiles, with its reminder status at a glance and a Configure Alert shortcut per row. A Sync Google Cal action sits on each appointment card.

Tab 2 — Patient profiles & vitals

The clinical picture the consultant will see: known allergies and sensitivities, active prescriptions and medications, medical conditions, emergency contact and guardian, ABHA ID and blood group where recorded, and a count of invoices settled.

Register Patient in the header creates an additional profile — for a child or an elderly parent — so one account can manage a family. The Active Patient Picker switches which profile the whole tab is showing.

Tab 3 — Historic records & tax invoices

Every past appointment, filterable by All, Upcoming / Active, Completed and Cancelled, with payment status per row and these actions:

  • Pay Online on anything still unpaid.
  • Tax Invoice — opens the printable receipt: hospital details, patient and consultant blocks, line items, subtotal, clinic tax and total settled. Print Bill sends it to the printer or a PDF.
  • Join Room for a video visit still in progress.
  • Feedback on completed visits — a 1-to-5 star rating and written comments on the consultant.
Two different money screens

The ₹50 booking receipt from step 4 and the consultation invoice here are separate documents. The booking receipt charges ₹50 with no GST; this table bills the consultant's fee with 18% GST added. A patient who has paid at the reception desk may still see UNPAID here until the desk settles the invoice in the system.

Cancelling

Cancelling asks for confirmation first, then releases the slot and triggers the waitlist alerts described in Part 1. The stated policy on the payment screen is free cancellation up to two hours before the appointment.

Part Five

Doctor manual

Consultant

Consultants get Persona Portal, Consultations, Clinical Patients and Document Vault. They cannot open the booking wizard — scheduling belongs to patients and the desk.

The consultation queue

Left rail → Care → Consultations

A two-column workspace. On the left, your queue — filtered to Today or Upcoming, cancellations hidden, sorted so anyone still to be seen floats above finished consultations, then by date and slot time. A counter reads “N waiting”. On the right, everything about the patient currently selected.

The right-hand panel gives you, before you say a word:

  • Age, contact, appointment type and the note the patient wrote at booking.
  • Known allergies, current medications and recorded conditions.
  • The last four completed visits.
  • Every document on file for them, newest first.

Writing the prescription

  1. Diagnosis

    One line — e.g. Stage 1 essential hypertension.

  2. Examination notes

    Findings, observations, vitals discussed.

  3. Prescription — one row per medicine

    Four columns per row: medicine, dose (40mg), frequency (Once daily, morning) and duration (30 days). Add medicine appends a row; the cross removes one. A running count sits above the rows.

  4. Advice & follow-up

    e.g. Low-salt diet, walk 30 min daily. Review in 12 weeks with a fresh lipid profile.

  5. Review & complete

    A review dialog shows the prescription exactly as the patient will receive it, with a print / save-as-PDF button. Esc backs out.

Allergy warning

The prescription block reads the patient's recorded allergies and warns beneath the medicine rows — “Allergic to Sulfa Drugs — check before prescribing.” It is an advisory only: it does not match against what you have typed and will not stop you saving. Keeping the allergy list in Clinical Patients accurate is what makes this warning worth anything.

What closing a consultation actually does

Four things, in order: the appointment is marked completed; every new medicine is appended to the patient's medication list; a dated summary of the diagnosis, notes and advice is appended to their clinical record; and the full prescription is filed into the Document Vault as Prescription — Dr. X — YYYY-MM-DD, which is what the patient downloads. The queue then advances to the next waiting patient automatically.

You cannot close a consultation empty — a diagnosis, a note, or at least one medicine is required.

Three ways to read your day

Left rail → Care → Persona Portal → Doctor Scheduler Dashboard

Slot-Wise Patients

Your published slots down the page, with the patients booked into each. The default, and the closest match to how an OPD actually runs.

Chronological Timeline

One continuous run of appointments in time order, regardless of slot boundaries.

Standard Checklist

A flat, tickable list — fastest for confirming that everyone has been seen.

Joining a video patient

Video appointments carry a Join Telehealth button in every view. Pressing it flips the appointment to active-video and opens the cabin.

Clinical Patients

Left rail → Records → Clinical Patients

The longitudinal record, searchable by name or phone. For each patient you can edit the demographics, and maintain three managed lists — allergies, medications and conditions — as removable tags rather than free text, plus a clinical notes field for surgical history and background.

Vitals tracking

Readings are charted across three metric groups you switch between:

  • BP & pulse — systolic, diastolic, pulse.
  • Glucose & SpO₂.
  • Haemoglobin.

Add a reading by date; delete any reading from the table.

Front Desk

The widest access in the building. The desk sees every rail destination except none — portal, consultations, care dashboard, booking, clinical patients and the vault — because it stands in for whoever is not at a screen.

Left rail → Care → Persona Portal → three tabs

Tab 1 — Clinic performance & revenue

Four headline figures across the top:

FigureCounts
Consulting revenueSum of settled invoices, in rupees
Total bookingsEvery booking that is not cancelled
Completed careAppointments the consultant has closed
Patient directoryDistinct profiles on file

Below them, two charts: Financial statement — total consultation inflows over time, and Capacity monitoring — appointment volumes by calendar date.

Tab 2 — Clinician availabilities

A roster table: specialist, consult fee, whether video is on, duty status (Active or On Leave) and the published slots as chips.

Update Availability opens an editor for three settings:

  • Consultation fee (₹) — the amount collected at reception, and the number the booking wizard quotes.
  • On-duty status — Active & booking available or On leave / temporarily blocked.
  • Telehealth video consultations — enabled, or in-person only. Turning this off greys out the video option in the booking wizard immediately.
Take care

These changes are live and apply to bookings made from that moment on. Existing bookings keep the fee and type they were made under.

Tab 3 — Onboard specialist

Adds a consultant to the roster: name, speciality, fee, biography, photo, whether video is offered, and the list of slots. A new consultant defaults to three patients per slot and to the standard set 09:00 AM · 10:00 AM · 02:00 PM · 04:00 PM if no slots are given. They become bookable straight away.

The Care Dashboard

Left rail → Care → Care Dashboard

Hospital-wide totals — appointments, active patients, documents held, and how many of those documents have been analysed — over a list of upcoming appointments. Each row offers:

  • Reschedule — opens the booking wizard as a modal, pre-filled (Part 3).
  • Cancel — with confirmation; releases the slot to its waitlist.
  • Share booking link & check-in QR — generates a scannable QR code and a copyable check-in link carrying the appointment and booking number, for the OPD registration desk or kiosk.

Alongside: a health-insights panel and a featured-specialists strip.

Collecting money

The desk collects the consultant's fee in person and can settle any unpaid invoice through the same checkout the patient sees, then print the receipt. The invoice applies the clinic tax rate the administrator has configured — 18% GST as shipped — on top of the consultation fee.

Application-level configuration

Care SMS triggers

Whether patient notifications fire on bookings and prescriptions. Demo No SMS gateway is connected in this build.

Telehealth rooms

Whether video consultations and diagnostic chat sessions are offered at all. Switching this off removes telehealth across the product regardless of individual consultant settings.

Save Global Configuration applies both, immediately, for every signed-in user.

Purge & seed system databases

Destructive — no undo

Purge & Seed System Databases wipes the live data and rewrites it with the demonstration set: seven consultants, four patients with full histories, and the sample appointments and documents. Every real booking, prescription, uploaded document and invoice is discarded.

Use it to reset a demonstration environment. Never use it on a system holding real patient records.

Part Eight

Document Vault & AI analysis

Patient Consultant Front Desk Admin

The only destination available to all four roles. Two tabs: Browse and Add document.

Left rail → Records → Document Vault

Browsing

Search runs across the whole document text — a medicine, a test value, a diagnosis, a doctor or a patient name will all find it. Category chips carry a live count each: All, Prescriptions, Lab tests, Referrals, Vaccinations, Other. A Patient dropdown narrows to one person, or Everyone. Every document can be downloaded or sent to a printer as a PDF. Documents that have been analysed carry an AI marker.

Adding a document

  1. Choose the patient

    The record it will be filed against.

  2. Title and category

    e.g. “Cardiology prescription — August 2026”.

  3. Paste the content

    The prescription, lab report or vaccination record as text. This text is what search reads and what the analyser is given.

What the analyser returns

Analyse sends the document text to Google Gemini and files six structured results against it:

FieldContents
SummaryUnder 100 words, in plain professional language.
Key findingsObservations, lab values and health statuses found in the text.
Extracted allergiesAllergies explicitly noted; empty if none.
Extracted medicationsMedicines prescribed or currently taken.
Suggested follow-upA recommended next test, action or lifestyle change.
Risk levelLow Medium High
Read this before relying on it

The analysis is a reading aid, not a clinical opinion. Nothing it extracts is written into the patient's allergy or medication lists automatically — a consultant still has to enter those in Clinical Patients. Analysis requires a configured Gemini API key; without one, the button returns an error and the document is stored unanalysed.

Part Nine

The telehealth room

Patient Consultant

The cabin opens automatically when either side joins a video appointment — it is not a destination you can navigate to cold. While a session is running, an In Session group appears in the left rail with a pulsing Live badge.

The call

Main video with the patient's self-view picture-in-picture, a running call timer, a LIVE indicator, and three controls: mute, camera off, and hang up. A live diagnostics log runs underneath.

Demo The feeds are simulated — no WebRTC connection is established.

Chat

A side panel for messages during the call, with a role switch so a single demonstrator can type as either the doctor or the patient, and an attach link control for sharing a titled URL — an ECG scan, a lab portal.

Notes & prescription

The consultant types findings and adds medicines one at a time while the call runs. These feed the same completion path as an in-person consultation.

Finishing

Ending the consultation shows a Consultation Finalized summary with the notes and prescribed medicines, a Download Prescription (PDF) button — a formatted, digitally-signed prescription sheet on hospital letterhead — and a way back to the portal.

Leaving the room without finishing (Exit Room) returns you to the portal with the appointment still active-video; you can rejoin. If you open the telehealth destination with no session running, you get a short explanation and a way back rather than an error.

Part Eleven

What is live, and what is staged

This build is a working demonstration. Some mechanisms run for real against the server; others are staged so the flow can be shown without external services. Presenting the second group as the first is the fastest way to lose a room — so here is the honest split.

Genuinely working

  • Slot capacity counting and enforcement at the server.
  • The waitlist: entries, cancellation triggers, alerts, and the ten-second poll.
  • Appointment state changes across every screen.
  • Patient profiles, clinical lists and vitals.
  • The Document Vault, and prescription filing on consultation close.
  • AI document analysis — a real Gemini call, with an API key configured.
  • Consultant roster, fees, slots and availability.
  • Printable invoices, receipts and prescription PDFs.

Staged for demonstration

  • Sign-in — four hard-coded accounts; no real authentication.
  • Payments — no gateway. Bookings are recorded as paid on confirmation.
  • Video calls — simulated feeds; no WebRTC connection.
  • WhatsApp, SMS, email and push — settings are captured, messages are not sent. Test buttons show on-screen toasts.
  • Delay and unavailability alerts — driven by the two simulate buttons.
  • Booking-step file uploads — file names only; use the vault for real documents.
  • Storage — a single JSON file, not a database.
The one thing to fix first

The API accepts every request without authentication. Anyone who can reach the server can read and modify all patient data. Put authentication in front of it before this build touches a public address or a real patient record.

Defects found while capturing these screenshots

Every screen in this manual was photographed from a running build rather than described from the code. Two things did not behave as the code suggested they would.

What happensWhyEffect
No OPD token on the confirmation screen The wizard generates a token and sends it, but the server never stores it, so nothing comes back to display. Patients have a booking ID but no queue token. The desk's QR pass invents one from the appointment ID instead.
A booking is recorded as paid before any money moves The server stamps every new appointment as paid at creation. Pay at the counter bookings look settled in the system while the ₹50 is still owed.
